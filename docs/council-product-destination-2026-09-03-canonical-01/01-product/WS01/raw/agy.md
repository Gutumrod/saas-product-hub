## Recommendation

WSM should be a **multi-tenant B2B supply-planning and dealer-allocation SaaS** whose single product identity is a **"demand-to-allocation source of truth"** for importers/distributors that sell scarce SKUs through a dealer network. The product is defined by the North Star `Demand → Supply → Gap → Allocation → Fulfillment`, and — critically, given how early the portfolio is — **the thin end-to-end loop that proves value is precisely the Phase 1 slice already approved in the frozen decision log**: Dealer booking → confirmed demand → manually entered reliable supply → explicit gap → manual/partial allocation → backorder → dealer-visible result.

The **core recurring operational pain** WSM exists to solve is that importers/distributors confuse *requested* quantity with *confirmed* supply and *allocated* with *fulfilled*, coordinating dealer demand, factory commitments, shortages and allocation across spreadsheets, chat and memory. The strongest product identity/endgame is not "an ERP-lite" or "an inventory system" — it is becoming the **one traceable, explainable record of who reliably wants what, which supply is trustworthy, where the shortage is, and who receives scarce supply when it changes** (North Star, 00_PRODUCT_VISION.md).

**Primary user/buyer:** a single operator/owner (or small owner/admin team) inside an importing/distributing business that (1) sources multiple SKUs from one or more suppliers/factories, (2) sells through multiple dealers, (3) routinely faces partial production, delay or shortage, and (4) currently allocates scarce supply manually. The *dealer* is a distinct user class (the demand-side actor submitting through the mobile booking link), but the **buyer is the importer/distributor operator**, not the dealer.

**Where V1 ends (recommended boundary):** V1 ends at **manual supply adapter → manual/partial allocation → backorder (explicit carry only) → dealer-visible self-scoped result**. V1 explicitly **does not** include: PO/factory-commitment/batch supply provenance (Phase 2), allocation *policies*/auto-allocation runs and promise revision history (Phase 3), inventory/warehouse/fulfillment ledger (Phase 4), dealer portal/deposits/payments/credit/invoices (Phase 5), risk/impact/notification automation (Phase 6), planning intelligence (Phase 7). Those are later phases and belong to later build briefs, not V1.

**Primary flows in V1:** (a) the owner/admin thin loop — tenant setup, catalogue/variant + supplier mapping, dealer creation, booking round creation, share booking link, watch demand aggregate, enter on-hand/incoming supply with confidence, review gap, allocate full/partial, review backorders and dealer result; and (b) the dealer journey — open mobile-first link, identify via tenant-issued dealer code (PD-008), see round items + closing time, submit quantities idempotently, receive explicit "not guaranteed supply" notice, and view only their own requested/allocated/waiting status.

**Multi-tenancy:** product-required, not merely architectural intent — the 00 vision, 01 PRD, 02 architecture, 03 security contract, 04 pricing and 10 roadmap all describe a multi-tenant SaaS, tenancy is enforced as server-authorized scoping and RLS, and the tenant is the fundamental isolation/entitlement unit. This is not optional plumbing.

**Recommendation on the locked invariants:** the *product-defining* invariants are the distinction `Requested ≠ Allocated ≠ Fulfilled` and `Ordered ≠ Confirmed ≠ Received` (00 principles 1–2), the Gap-not-double-counted semantics, audit immutability, tenant/dealer isolation, and explicit (non-automatic) backorder carry. The *implementation-detail* invariants (specific column types, RLS policy shapes, advisory-lock mechanics) are evidence of the contract being internally consistent — a prerequisite — but are not themselves the product answer.

## Key reasons

1. **Phase 1's thin end-to-end loop and the North Star align exactly, and this is deliberate (PD-004, principle 6).** The roadmap, PRD, UX flows, and Phase 1 schema all describe the *same* slice: dealer booking → demand → manual reliable supply → gap → manual/partial allocation → backorder → dealer result. This is a product decision (PD-004: "thin end-to-end loop, not a deep isolated module"), and it is the correct product answer because the pain is a *cross-stage coordination* pain, not a single-module pain. Building a deep PO module (Phase 2) or a deep allocation-engine module (Phase 3) before the loop connects demand→allocation would prove far less of the core value.

2. **The product-defining distinction is the semantic separation of requested/allocated/fulfilled and ordered/confirmed/received.** This is WSM's reason to exist: today operators conflate these states and misallocate scarce supply on gut+spreadsheets. The whole model (derived allocation, backorder lifecycle, supply-confidence classes, audit immutability, canonical labeling rule in UX) enforces this separation. Everything else is in service of making that distinction traceable and auditable.

3. **The buyer and the dealer are clearly distinguished, and V1's dealer surface is deliberately thin and mobile-first.** The buyer signs up and configures; the dealer only books by link and reads their own result. This keeps V1 small, keeps dealer friction near zero (no account setup — a tenant-issued code, PD-008), and proves demand capture quickly. It also means the mobile-first NFR (NFR-001) is a first-class requirement, not an afterthought, because the dealer journey *is* the external-facing proof point.

4. **Multi-tenancy is product-required, not aspirational architecture.** The tenant is the isolation, entitlement, policy and currency unit everywhere (FR-TEN-001, SEC-TEN-001, RLS, tenant_policies, base_currency THB PD-010). This is a real deployment and commercial shape (multiple importing businesses each with their own dealers/rounds), so tenancy cannot be deferred — but it also must be cheap to do correctly *once* so V1 doesn't rebuild it. The schema contract already encodes the correct scoping patterns (verified-JWT tenant claim, dealer-scoped secondary policy, narrow booking-link role), which de-risks V1.

5. **The locked invariants that are product-defining are few and testable; the rest is implementation detail.** Product-defining: requested≠allocated≠fulfilled, ordered≠confirmed≠received, gap-not-double-counted, audit immutability, dealer self-scoping, explicit-backorder-carry, many-to-many sourcing. Implementation detail: exact column types, RLS function mechanics, advisory-lock granularity, view formulas. Recognising this split means the Phase 1 build brief should be judged on the former, not re-litigated on the latter.

6. **The current state is genuinely "documentation-locked, not built" and the next gate is correctly a build brief.** No migration/scaffold/deploy exists; schema contract (with the Gap double-count defect already corrected) is authoritative; owner decisions PD-007–011 resolve the remaining shape questions. This means the product answer is well-constrained and the risk is execution/evidence discipline, not ambiguity about what to build.

7. **Module Hub fit is promising but must not import shared infrastructure into V1.** The brief explicitly warns not to assume shared infrastructure belongs in V1. The only candidate directory found at this root is `apps/hub-web` (+ a `booking-ticket-module` product), not a mature shared-runtime catalog; given placement/billing are still pending portfolio decisions, V1 should build on the schema contract's own scoping (which is deliberately neutral to shared-vs-dedicated hosting) rather than coupling to unproven shared infra.

## Risks / failure cases

- **R1 – Semantics collapse in the build (highest product risk).** If any tier labels requested stock as "reserved" or factory-ordered as "incoming confirmed" (explicitly forbidden in 06 UX principles), the entire value proposition collapses and users start trusting wrong numbers. Mitigation: canonical labeling enforced in domain terms + G4/G5/G6 negative tests already mandated.
- **R2 – Gap double-count regression.** The historical design carried a Gap double-count defect (DOC-001) that the authoritative schema contract already corrected. If a builder re-derives from the old `design/04` evidence (which still carries the defective version), shortage numbers double. Mitigation: PHASE1_SCHEMA.md is explicitly *the* authority and the defect is flagged; but the risk persists because the stale evidence still exists in the repo.
- **R3 – Over-allocation / concurrency failure.** Allocation is manual but concurrent (REL-001, G6). Without the advisory lock and transactional invariant enforcement, two admins could over-allocate a source, or allocation could exceed requested qty. This is a financial/trust failure for the buyer. Mitigation: G6 gate + transactional invariants + negative-path test (allocation race, allocation over reliable supply).
- **R4 – Backorder semantics drift (double counting on the demand side).** Backorders are one-open-per-line, no auto-carry (PD-011). If anyone treats backorder qty as an additive separate shortage on top of open_confirmed_demand, totals inflate. This is the mirror of R2 and must be tested (G5).
- **R5 – Tenancy/isolation leak.** Dealer self-scoping, cross-tenant substitution, and the anonymous booking-link path are the attack surface. Failure = one dealer reads another's demand/allocation, or one tenant sees another's data. Mitigation: G3 negative-test baseline (already mandatory), RLS with verified-JWT claim, FK-check rejections.
- **R6 – Over-scoping V1.** Pulling PO/commitment (Phase 2) or allocation policy runs / future-supply allocation (Phase 3) into V1 would defeat the thin-loop proof and multiply scope. Mitigation: explicit non-goals and phase gate.
- **R7 – Treating doc lock as build approval.** Documentation Lock (PD-012) is explicitly *not* implementation approval. Authorizing a migration/scaffold without an approved Phase 1 build brief would bypass the MASTER_CHECKLIST implementation gate.
- **R8 – Idempotency/replay abuse on booking.** Dealer retry (network interruption) must not double-insert demand (FR-BKG-003, idempotency_key per round+dealer+key). Failure = inflated demand → wrong gap → wrong allocation. Mitigation: G4 + negative replay test.
- **R9 – Retention/legal launch blockers.** Retention periods and SLA/support wording are flagged as launch blockers requiring business/legal approval; engineering must not invent them. Shipping V1 publicly without them violates the release rule.
- **R10 – Placement/billing uncertainty.** Runtime/db placement and central billing are pending portfolio decisions. If the Phase 1 build embeds a placement or billing assumption, it may need rework. The schema contract deliberately avoids this; the risk is if the implementer over-assumes.

## Open questions / missing evidence

- **Market/competitive grounding.** No market-size, competitive-set, or demand-validation evidence for this exact segment (importer/distributor scarce-SKU dealer allocation) is present in the reviewed docs. Whether the pain is large/willing-to-pay enough to justify the build is unanswered by this repo. (Out of this gate's mandate to decide, but flagged as a gap the portfolio owner should close separately.)
- **Who is the pilot/design partner, and does WSM have a first real tenant?** PD-010 assumes first tenant is THB-based, but no named or committed first customer is documented. A thin loop is only "proven" against a real importer/distributor's workflow and real dealer friction — that evidence does not exist yet.
- **Actual dealer count/SKU count/SKU scarcity profile per tenant.** The allocation/scarcity hypothesis (who gets scarce supply) presumes meaningful SKU scarcity and dealer count. No current-volume or per-tenant scale data in the docs.
- **Exact success thresholds.** KPI *definitions* exist (07 spec) but no target/acceptance numbers for "proven" (e.g., what allocation-coverage or demand-capture rate constitutes success for the V1 thin loop). The roadmap/status do not define a measurable exit criterion for the Phase 1 build brief to be judged against — this should be settled in that brief.
- **Backorder carry semantics at round completion for the *dealer-visible* picture.** PD-011 says no auto-carry and the backorder stays `open`; but how a dealer sees a "waiting" line post-round (before carry) is not described in UX doc detail. Minor, but an edge case the build brief must pin down.
- **Supply provenance depth.** V1 records source/confidence/effective_date on manual supply; the exact set of provenance fields a Phase 2 migration needs to "replace the adapter without reinterpreting history" is not fully enumerated (only design intent). A contract for the adapter's immutability boundary is worth settling before schema ships.
- **Entitlement snapshot sync timing/failure mode.** `entitlement_snapshots` exists, but the refresh cadence and fail-safe on billing-boundary outage (REL-002) aren't specified beyond "cannot self-upgrade / fail safe." Operational detail for the build brief.
- **Reporting/analytics surface for V1.** 07 defines events/KPIs but not which dashboards ship in V1 vs later. Whether "gap.viewed" or a bare gap table is the V1 reporting answer is unresolved.
- **Module Hub fit depth.** Only a light scan was performed; whether the hub actually hosts reusable tenancy/booking components that could de-risk WSM is unresolved and should be investigated before the Phase 1 build brief commits to a hosting/scoping posture (but not pulled into V1 purely for convenience).
