## Recommendation
**Credible-but-unproven recurring paid market: conditionally plausible, pilot WTP evidence is mandatory before final prices.** The Pain→Capability→Outcome→Reason-to-Pay chain is structurally coherent and the ฿490–฿990 reference band sits inside a verified Thai price envelope (OneRun ฿299 → EikQueue ฿590 → SeeU ฿990–1,990 → LINE OA Basic 1,280). However: (1) direct Thai competitor EikQueue sells deposit collection + slip upload + LINE OA notifications at ฿590/mo with UNLIMITED staff — undercutting BK01 Basic and Pro on both price and staff entitlement; (2) BK01's own differentiation hypothesis concedes LINE/PromptPay alone are not defensible, and the combination loop is already partially matched in-market; (3) willingness-to-pay, retention, no-show reduction, and deposit reliability are UNMEASURED; (4) Pro auto-slip unit economics are unresolved and could consume a large share of ฿990 revenue if mis-scoped. The market hypothesis is credible; the business case is not yet bankable. Recommendation to Codex: market gate can only be rated credible with a pilot-evidence precondition, not on current evidence.

## Key reasons

0. **Pain → Capability → Outcome → Business Value → Reason to Pay chain (explicit):**
   - **Pain** (from locked vision): Thai 1–10-provider shops coordinate availability via LINE/DM + manual calendars + staff memory; failure modes = double-booked slots, no-shows on deposit-required services, endless manual confirm/follow-up. Financially felt daily.
   - **Capability**: collision-safe scheduling enforced at the authoritative data layer + PromptPay-native deposit/slip state machine + merchant-owned LINE notifications + low-burden Thai-first setup.
   - **Outcome**: only genuinely available times are bookable; high-intent customers pre-commit a deposit before occupying a slot; confirmations/reminders go out automatically; staff schedules stay consistent.
   - **Business Value**: fewer lost slots and no-shows (revenue protection), reduced manual verification/follow-up labor (time savings), fewer double-booking incidents (reputation protection).
   - **Reason to Pay**: one prevented no-show on a ฿500–2,000 service ≈ one month of Basic (฿490); the auto-slip workload relief is the concrete Pro (฿990) trigger. The chain is structurally coherent; every quantitative link (no-show rate, conversion, retention) is UNMEASURED until pilot — that is the honest state of this gate.
1. **The payer is well-defined and reachable**: single-location shop owner-operator (1–10 providers) in Thai hair/barber/beauty/nail. This is the same buyer Thai incumbents monetize today (SeeU, EikQueue, OneRun), so payer existence and channel are validated by parallel businesses — not yet for BK01 itself.
2. **The outcome worth recurring payment is deposit-integrity + scheduling-integrity, not "booking"**: the monetizable outcome is fewer lost slots (collision-safe availability), no-show cost transfer (deposit before booking), and reduced manual confirm/follow-up. One prevented no-show on a typical ฿500–2,000 salon service ≈ 1 month of Basic. That arithmetic makes ฿490/mo rational — but it is a hypothesis until pilot data shows real no-show rates and deposit behavior.
3. **Price-band plausibility is externally anchored**: Thai incumbents charge ฿299 (OneRun), ฿590 (EikQueue Pro), ฿990 (SeeU base), ฿1,990 (SeeU Premium); global entrants price in THB locally (Fresha ฿525 independent). ฿490 Basic / ฿990 Pro sit inside the observed Thai willingness-to-pay envelope. The band is defensible; the specific point prices are not yet.
4. **Competitive undercutting is real, not theoretical**: EikQueue Pro at ฿590/mo includes unlimited staff (vs BK01 Pro's 10-staff cap at ฿990), deposit collection + slip upload, LINE OA notifications with the identical merchant-borne LINE cost model, multi-branch at ฿1,290. A Thai buyer doing due diligence will find it. BK01 cannot win a feature/price comparison on V1 scope alone; it must win on scheduling integrity at the data layer, verification quality, and setup burden — all currently unproven in-market.
5. **Differentiation is execution-thin, not structural**: SlipOK's open API/LINE-webhook means any competitor can add auto-slip verification in days; LINE OA integration is commodity (9+ Thai systems have it). BK01's vision correctly admits this. The remaining defensible candidates — authoritative-layer collision safety, Thai-native deposit state machine, low setup burden — are engineering claims requiring pilot proof, not marketing claims.
6. **Pro's paid differentiator has viable but unresolved COGS**: SlipOK-class pricing (฿0.30–0.70/verification overage; ฿600/mo for a 1,000-slip pack) means a Pro merchant doing ~300 deposits/mo implies ฿90–210/mo verification COGS — 9–21% of ฿990 revenue at overage rates, or a bundled pack that could consume up to 60% if mis-scoped. The missing allowance/top-up decision is therefore a business-critical gate, not a detail: it determines whether Pro is profitable or loss-making at scale.
7. **Entitlement structure aligns with value in shape, weakly in point**: deposit automation (Basic manual → Pro auto) as the paid ladder matches the workload gradient; removing booking-count walls is correct for this ICP. But the 10-staff Pro cap vs EikQueue unlimited-staff at 60% of the price is a structural weakness; and 14-day trials are standard (EikQueue 14d, SimplyBook 14d, Fresha 7d) while SeeU's 30 days is more generous.
8. **The free-alternative threat is severe because LINE-native features are converging**: LINE OA Basic (฿1,280/mo) already bundles customer profiles + reminders + scheduled messaging; Free OA gives 300 msgs/mo. Merchants can approximate part of BK01's loop inside LINE for ฿0–1,280. BK01's counter is that LINE does not do collision-safe multi-provider scheduling or deposit state management — the operations layer — but the burden of proof is on demonstrated reliability.

## Risks / failure cases
1. **Undercut-and-copy**: EikQueue (or SeeU) adds SlipOK-based auto-verification and prices at ฿690–890 — BK01's Pro differentiator evaporates while its price sits 43–100% above the nearest Thai alternative. Probability: moderate; SlipOK integration is publicly sold today.
2. **Status-quo reversion**: merchant uses Trial, likes it, but reverts to LINE + spreadsheet because (a) LINE OA Free/Basic already covers reminders/profiles, (b) PromptPay-to-own-account receiving costs ฿0, (c) merchant-owned LINE means BK01 holds no communication lock-in. Cancellation is frictionless by design (CSV export, monthly billing, data retention) — good for trust, bad for retention.
3. **Auto-slip unit-economics failure**: Pro allowance set too high (e.g., 1,000 verifications bundled at ฿990 with ฿600 pack COGS) → Pro margin ≈ negative after Stripe fees; set too low → merchants hit the wall and perceive Pro as a quota product, reviving the retired booking-limit perception.
4. **LINE cost stack rejection**: production path requires merchant to ALSO pay LINE OA Basic ฿1,280/mo for adequate message volume → real stack cost for Basic merchant ≈ ฿1,770/mo, approaching SeeU Premium territory and doubling the perceived price. Trial (central OA, WSTERA-borne) masks this cost until conversion — a trial-to-paid conversion killer.
5. **Unmeasured-value marketing trap**: selling before pilot evidence forces generic "booking system" messaging, colliding head-on with cheaper incumbents; without no-show/deposit-reliability data there is no reason-to-pay narrative beyond features.
6. **Support-cost trap at ฿490 ARPU**: Thai SMB SaaS at low ARPU with high-touch onboarding expectations (low setup burden is a core promise) can be support-unprofitable; EikQueue/SeeU already operate this segment and their survival indicates margins are possible but tight.
7. **Market-timing risk**: V1 sellable state is not reached (DB gates BLOCKED_ENVIRONMENT, provider behaviors unverified) — the business window is open for incumbents to close the auto-slip gap before BK01 launches.
