## Recommendation
DocCraft has a **credible but narrow and already-contested paid market**. The willingness-to-pay for exactly this wedge is *proven by precedent*: BillKub sells a nearly identical no-login Thai document tool (guest mode, local IndexedDB, quotation/invoice/receipt free; tax invoice, logo, no watermark, quote→invoice→receipt behind Pro) at **฿299 one-time lifetime** with a 7-day money-back guarantee. That validates the one-time purchase model — and simultaneously occupies DocCraft's niche. DocCraft's remaining defensible ground is measurable **tax-correctness depth**: withholding tax (WHT) with eligible-line basis and proportional discount allocation, conditional tax invoice validity gating (VAT-registered profile + required fields), deposit derivation, deterministic rounding, and A4 print fidelity — none of which BillKub advertises and which FlowAccount/PEAK gate behind paid tiers or bundle inside a full accounting system. Model fit: **one-time purchase ฿299–599 (primary) or hybrid (perpetual base + optional cloud/Pro add-on post-MVP)**; pure subscription is a poor fit — V1 has no retention driver and free-forever anchors exist at ฿0. Sustainable business beyond a small one-time base requires the Post-MVP cloud/Pro tier, which is correctly gated behind Pilot Validation.

## Key reasons
- **Payer = user = Thai micro-business owner/operator (Q1).** They pay for an *outcome*: fast, correct, print-ready Thai tax documents (VAT/WHT/deposit/tax invoice) without adopting Word/Excel discipline or a full accounting system. No buyer-user split exists in V1 — pricing must fit a personal expense, not a company procurement budget (favors ฿299–599 one-time over ฿199+/mo subscriptions).
- **Model fit (Q2):** one-time fits occasional-use with no retention driver; hybrid (perpetual + future cloud add-on) matches the Post-MVP gate; pure subscription over ~฿100/mo is indefensible when FlowAccount Free (unlimited docs) and PEAK Freelance (free) sit at ฿0 and BillKub Pro is ฿299 *lifetime*. Usage-based pricing is irrelevant for a local, no-backend tool.
- **Competitive reality (Q3/Q4):** the "no-accounting document tool" niche in Thailand is no longer empty — BillKub already runs the exact no-login + local-storage + PromptPay playbook with a one-time price. FlowAccount Free covers "unlimited documents free" for users who tolerate login + watermark. PEAK Freelance covers free-but-capped accounting. Indirect status quo (Word/Excel templates) is free and entrenched; international tools (Zoho/Wave) are free but not Thai-tax-localized.
- **Measurable differentiation (Q5)** — not generic feature language: (a) WHT with eligible-line basis + proportional document-discount allocation — absent in BillKub, gated to paid tiers in FlowAccount; (b) conditional tax invoice gating on VAT-registered profile + required-field validity — a correctness guarantee no free competitor advertises; (c) zero watermark — vs FlowAccount Free and BillKub Free; (d) no account at all — vs every Thai SaaS; (e) deterministic centralized rounding + deposit derivation — auditable correctness vs hand-computed Excel. Caveat: each is a feature, not a moat; BillKub could add WHT quickly.
- **Retention/upgrade (Q6):** V1's honest answer is *weak* — document creation is episodic; local storage gives no multi-device pull. Real upgrade triggers (cloud sync, document history, quote→invoice→receipt, e-sign) are exactly the Post-MVP Phase 7+ items gated behind Pilot Validation — the gate structure is correct; the business case inside V1 alone is a one-time-purchase base with no recurring revenue.
- **Cost/support/margin (Q7):** COGS near zero (static frontend, no backend) → gross margin excellent. Real costs are (a) distribution/SEO/content to reach micro-businesses, (b) support burden for local-storage data-loss complaints ("my documents disappeared"), (c) maintaining Thai tax rules across RD changes (WHT rates, VAT), (d) building a checkout rail — V1 explicitly excludes billing infra, so *collecting money is itself unbuilt scope*.
- **Pain→Capability→Outcome→Business Value→Reason to Pay (Q9):** Pain: VAT-registered micro-sellers must issue correct tax invoices/WHT docs but fear getting tax math wrong and won't adopt accounting software. Capability: no-login browser studio with deterministic Thai VAT/WHT/deposit logic and A4 print. Outcome: correct, clean (unwatermarked), print-ready tax documents in minutes, offline. Business value: fewer corrected/redone documents, credible documents for clients and for filing, zero software adoption cost. Reason to pay: remove watermark, guarantee tax-document correctness, own the tool outright — at a price below one accounting-software month.

## Risks / failure cases
- **FlowAccount Free "good enough" trap:** if micro-users tolerate watermark + login, DocCraft's wedge collapses; watermark only matters to VAT-registered sellers needing clean formal tax invoices (share UNVERIFIED).
- **BillKub price anchor:** ฿299 lifetime for most of DocCraft's V1 feature set caps what buyers will pay; DocCraft must justify a premium purely on WHT/tax-invoice correctness depth — a thin moat that BillKub can copy in a minor release.
- **No money-collection path in V1:** selling requires a checkout (PromptPay/QR/static link) — unbuilt by definition; time-to-revenue depends on it.
- **Local-storage fragility as a trust barrier:** a paying user who loses documents after a browser wipe becomes a detractor; "convenience persistence, not backup" is honest but commercially weak.
- **e-Tax direction risk:** if Thai e-Tax invoice obligations tighten for small sellers, DocCraft's exclusion of e-Tax becomes a *disqualifier* rather than a scope choice (timeline UNVERIFIED).
- **One-time revenue ceiling:** no recurring revenue in V1; LTV = one ฿299–599 payment minus CAC from content/SEO — the business only survives volume or the Post-MVP cloud tier.
- **Remediation dependency:** Product Gate = REMEDIATE (Phases 4.1/5/6 unfinished); no market verdict matters until logo, PromptPay QR, and hardening actually ship — the sellable V1 does not exist yet.
